Region growing across a raster scan

The 8×8 image is scanned left to right, then row by row, to find the next pixel to look at:

  1. Already had its own neighbours tested — nothing new here, move on.
  2. Not a seed and not yet in a region — nothing to do, move on.
  3. A seed with no region yet — start a new region here, then test its neighbours.
  4. Already in a region, but not yet tested itself — test its neighbours now, to keep the region growing.

A neighbour is accepted only if it is still unclaimed and the region's max − min stays within the threshold after adding it. Accepted neighbours that are still ahead of the scan simply wait for their own turn. But a neighbour the scan has already passed — up or left of the current pixel — would never get a turn of its own, so it is tested immediately instead. A pixel turned down by one region can still be picked up by a different region later in the scan.

A pixel like (0,0) is skipped when the scan first reaches it — it isn't a seed and has no region yet. Moments later, region A's own neighbour test claims it anyway. Since the scan can never go back to (0,0) for its own turn, (0,0) is expanded on the spot, which is how (1,0) ends up already inside region A by the time the scan reaches it — exactly as it should.
